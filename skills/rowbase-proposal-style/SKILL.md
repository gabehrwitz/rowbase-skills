# Rowbase Proposal & Exec Writing Style

Write so the reader feels a competent person is talking to them and making a
case they can trust. Warm, persuasive, plain. Every claim earns its place with
a specific fact, number, or example. Cut anything that sounds like a template.

This skill covers three modes. The voice is the same across all three; only the
structure and formality shift.

## The Voice (all modes)

- **Lead with the reader's benefit, not your features.** Say what they get and
  why it matters to them, then how you deliver it.
- **Warm, not stiff.** Write like you'd talk to a smart client across a table.
  Contractions are fine. Read it aloud; if a sentence sounds like something a
  person would only ever type, rewrite it.
- **Be specific or be quiet.** Back every claim with a number, a named example,
  or a concrete consequence — in the same or next sentence. If you can't, cut
  the claim.
- **Confident, not loud.** State strengths plainly. Restraint reads as
  competence; hype reads as insecurity.
- **Make the argument visible.** The reader should always know what you're
  claiming and why it's true.

## Modes

### Proposals & competitive bids
- Answer the actual question / rubric item first, in plain language, before any
  framing. Evaluators skim — give them the answer, then the support.
- Map to how it'll be judged. If there's a scoring rubric or evaluation
  criteria, structure around it and make each requirement easy to find.
- Prove capability with evidence: track record, specifics, named outcomes — not
  adjectives about yourself.
- Address the obvious risk or objection before they raise it. Owning it builds
  more trust than hiding it.

### Executive comms (memos, board/investor updates, strategy briefs)
- Put the decision, ask, or headline in the first two sentences. Then support.
- One idea per paragraph. Make it skimmable: the reader should get the spine
  from topic sentences alone.
- Quantify. "Pipeline grew" is noise; "pipeline grew 38% QoQ to $2.1M" is
  signal.
- Name the tradeoff or the risk. Leadership trusts writing that shows its work.

### Client-facing business writing (emails, SOWs, follow-ups, pitches)
- Relationship first. Be human and direct; get to the point fast and respect
  their time.
- One clear ask or next step per message. Make it obvious what happens next.
- Match the channel: short and plain for email; structured for an SOW; no
  markdown tables in a casual note.

## Stamp These Out (every mode)

**AI-slop tells:**
- "Not just X, but Y" as a default move.
- Over-polished three-part lists and robotic symmetry.
- Em-dash overuse and identical sentence shapes back to back.
- "It's worth noting," "in today's landscape," "at the end of the day."

**Corporate filler & hype:**
- Buzzwords and "best-in-class," "world-class," "cutting-edge," "synergy."
- Fake urgency and manufactured stakes.
- Throat-clearing intros that delay the point.
- Big claims you can't defend in the next sentence.

**Unsupported claims:**
- Any assertion without a specific fact, number, or example behind it. Either
  add the proof or cut the sentence.

**Over-formality & stiffness:**
- Bureaucratic hedging ("it is anticipated that," "please be advised").
- Passive voice where active is clearer.
- Formal register where plain speech would land harder.

## Workflow

1. State the single point of the piece to yourself in one plain sentence.
2. Identify the reader and what they need: a decision, a yes, reassurance, a
   next step.
3. Draft the answer/benefit first, then the support.
4. Pass for proof: every claim has a fact, number, or example — or it's cut.
5. Read it aloud. Rewrite anything stiff, generic, or grandiose.
6. Final slop check against the lists above.

## Output Check

Before returning anything the reader may act on:
- Does the first paragraph carry the point, ask, or benefit?
- Could the reader skim topic sentences and still get the argument?
- Does every claim have proof attached?
- Did you cut filler without sanding off the warmth or the point?
- Does it sound like a sharp human, not a template?
- Is the formatting right for the channel?
